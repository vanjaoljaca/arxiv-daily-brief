# Today’s useful questions — arXiv, 2026-07-13

*Selected public excerpts from the revised edition.*

You do not need to read any of these papers. The point is to extract what the researchers learned, what that should change, and which questions are worth carrying into education, product design, language, and programming work.

The strongest thread today was not “AI got smarter.” It was that **the shape of an interface or learning activity changes what people notice, understand, select, and trust**.

## Learning and education

### [Micro-level AI Feedback Features and Student Responses in Consecutive LLM Tutoring Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08952)

In more than sixteen thousand real student–tutor exchanges, worked examples, analogies, and concrete comparisons were followed by more signs of understanding and less repeated confusion. Longer replies were independently associated with less understanding, while empathic wording had no measurable relationship with those outcomes.

**Update:** supportive tutoring is not the same as effective tutoring. When a learner is stuck, change the representation before adding more prose.

**Question:** How could a tutor detect that the learner needs a different representation rather than a longer answer?

## Language

### [Conceptual Networks for Cross-Linguistic Idiomatic Expressions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09576)

Across idiomatic expressions in eight languages, phrases grouped more strongly by underlying concepts—containment, concealment, social roles, emotional valence—than by language. Those explicit concepts also identified plausible translations better than ordinary embedding similarity alone.

**Update:** figurative meaning is not always best represented by statistical proximity. Idioms may be easier to learn as families of shared metaphors than as isolated phrases.

**Question:** Where do languages choose radically different images for the same social situation?

## UX and interaction

### [Central Tendency Bias in Human Selection of AI-Generated Design Variations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09018)

When people saw a more varied set of generated designs, they became more likely to choose designs near the visual center. Producing diverse options did not produce diverse selections; the gallery itself pulled judgment toward its average.

**Update:** a variation wall is not a neutral container. Simultaneous presentation can quietly filter diversity back out.

**Question:** Is my option wall helping users explore possibilities, or teaching them to choose the average of whatever I showed?

## Programming and testing

### [How Far Are We from Detecting Flaky Tests? On the Limits of Code-Based Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09345)

Several detectors looked strong because their benchmarks contained shortcuts. Once those were removed, code alone often could not reveal flakiness. In real CI cases, most flaky end-to-end tests needed execution evidence beyond test code and logs to identify the cause.

**Update:** flakiness is often a relationship between code, timing, state, dependencies, and environment—not a property sitting inside the test file.

**Question:** What environmental state must be captured so a future failure is explainable rather than merely repeatable?
